# Version 0.9 corrected serial/two-slot A/B — 2026-08-30

This is the corrected hardware-presentation result for the private 2017-iMac
display appliance. Unlike the earlier directional A/B, a drawable with
`presentedTime == 0` is counted as a drop rather than being assigned a fallback
callback time. The display was the native `2560 × 1440` HiDPI point space backed
by `5120 × 2880` pixels in a 60 Hz mode. The transport was lossless 8-bit
BGRA-to-TBD2/DPCM, not HEVC, NV12, AI reconstruction, or downsampling.

## Corrected result

Both five-minute runs used the same project-owned full-screen Retina target. It
updates a one-physical-pixel grid, fine black/white edges, full-resolution RGB
bars, and text from a 60 Hz timer. The receiver was intentionally terminated
after each measurement so it emitted the bounded session summary, then its
launch agent restored the normal desktop.

| Receiver | Source tick rate | Presented FPS | Presented | Drops | Gap p95 | Gap p99 | Maximum gap | Payload | Integrity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Serial, one 64 MiB receive slot | 59.993 Hz | 58.376 | 19,417 | 55 | 16.8 ms | 33.4 ms | 616.741 ms | 6.200 Gbit/s | zero malformed/queue/GPU/renderer/order errors |
| Bounded overlap, two 64 MiB slots | 59.983 Hz | **59.972** | 17,943 | **1** | **16.8 ms** | **16.8 ms** | **33.376 ms** | 6.434 Gbit/s | zero malformed/queue/GPU/renderer/order errors |

The clean overlap session prefetched 17,943 DPCM packets. Average prefetch wait
was 0.725 ms and maximum wait was 25.888 ms. Its single `prefetchAborts=1`
occurred when the test deliberately sent SIGTERM after the animation; there was
no abort during playback. The summary therefore uses `reason=shutdown-requested`
even though the measured stream and automatic post-test recovery passed.

One earlier overlap attempt is deliberately excluded from the comparison. It
contained a measured 22.273-second idle interval between first presentation and
the motion target, which reduced its aggregate result to 55.318 FPS while its
p99 was already 16.9 ms. Repeating immediately after display admission removed
that test-harness artifact and produced the row above.

The receiver changed by 808 KiB across the short post-warm-up window. Its

duration limitation.

## Human acceptance and decision

The owner physically observed native Retina text, colors, the 5K motion target,
normal clicking, and exactly one cursor. They described the experience as the
best of the tested software-display options, better than their Luna experience,
with no objectionable latency and only a small cadence difference before this
optimization. That is human usability evidence, not a hardware-zero-latency
claim.

The two-slot mode is selected and left enabled. It raises the receiver's fixed
receive pool from 64 MiB to 128 MiB—negligible on this 40 GB iMac—without adding
an unbounded frame queue or disk cache. Adding more queued frames is not assumed
to be better because it can increase input-to-display latency.

## Lifecycle acceptance after selection

The installed two-slot candidate also passed two physical recovery checks. A
real MacBook display sleep/wake paused the iMac surface and restored the same
extended desktop after source wake. A later physical Thunderbolt unplug/replug
briefly showed the receiver's bounded “connection paused” state while macOS
rebuilt the bridge; without any relaunch or new permission prompt, it then
restored one established direct-link stream and one online 5120 × 2880 display.
At the final check there was one sender process, one sender window, one receiver
process/listener, and one macOS virtual display.

## Result

This run **fails the strict receiver-memory release gate**. It is useful
diagnostic evidence, not a production-release qualification.

Receiver RSS continued in a regular roughly 80–84 KiB-per-minute staircase
after every tested warm-up exclusion. Its fitted slope was +5.204 MiB/hour
after 600 seconds, +4.819 after 1,200, +4.807 after 1,800, and +4.823 after
2,400. That persistence rejects the hypothesis that version 0.8 was merely
finishing a short allocator warm-up. The sender, descriptor counts, thread
counts, and all measured on-disk directories plateaued.

## Session-state finding

The iMac became securely locked before this run. The receiver continued to
accept and render incoming work while its Metal presentation surface could no
longer complete. Its end-of-session counter reported 215,747 DPCM packets,
zero completed presentations, 215,745 presentation drops, and a
`presentation-callback-timeout`. This explains the physical black screen plus
local cursor and also shows why the appliance must pause capture and GPU work
while the receiver has no usable Aqua surface.

After the sender was unloaded, the receiver returned to 0% CPU and retained
only its connection-availability system-idle assertion. It held no
`UserIsActive` or display-sleep assertion. `/usr/bin/leaks` reported zero
detectable leaks (with the normal restricted-process caveat). `vmmap` showed
the expected fixed receive buffers and bounded Metal/IOSurface pools.

## Disposition

Version 0.8 remains the last physically demonstrated full-frame baseline, but
it is not the release candidate. The next candidate adds an explicit,
epoch-ordered source/receiver lifecycle handshake so that lock, screensaver,
display sleep, and wake act like a real external monitor: preserve the virtual
display arrangement and control connection, blank safely, stop frame/GPU/audio
production, and resume only after a fresh current-generation frame is actually
presented.
